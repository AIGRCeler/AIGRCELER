# AIGRCeler Discover

`aig` means `py -3 .claude/shared/engine/aigrceler.py`. Add `-p <system>` to commands about one AI system.

## Purpose

Know the system well enough that the risk assessment is accurate and the controls fit. Output: a discovery record in which every fact has a source, a tier, a proposed lane confirmed by AI Governance, and an honest list of what is not known.

Discover works on one system at a time. It does not sweep the company for AI tools nobody told you about. If a person mentions a tool that is not registered, register it as its own system and carry on.

## The discovery profile

The profile is a fixed set of questions in eleven sections: A purpose and ownership, B people, C decisions and human control, D data, E model and technology, F testing and performance, G security and access, H dependencies and fallback, I harm and impact, J legal and regulatory context, K existing governance. It is built into the engine; `aig profile show` lists every question with its answer, source and status, and `00-status/discovery-record.md` is the same thing as a document.

How much is asked depends on the lane: the light lane asks the core questions, the standard lane adds the standard ones, the full lane asks them all. Some questions apply only when a fact calls for them (for example, training data questions only when the company trained or adapted a model). The count shown by the tool is the count for this system.

Sixteen questions are critical: they decide the tier, the lane or a risk floor. They can never be recorded as "not known".

Every answer is recorded with who said it (or which document), when, and the earlier versions. Documents the system should have (model card, vendor contract, data-processing agreement, test report, and similar) are noted in a register: `aig profile doc --kind ... --name ... --status ... --source ...`.

## Steps

1. Atlas registers the system (`aig project create`) and records what the person has already said. The screening values are fixed words: `--users few|many|very-many`, `--autonomy reviewed|sampled|automated`, `--data none|personal|sensitive`, `--impact minor|business|rights-or-safety`. Record only what a person told you or a document says. Never fill a fact in from a guess or from the system's name. If you must infer something to move on, say so (`--inferred`): it is kept, but stays open until a person confirms it.
2. See what is open: `aig profile show --open`, or `aig stage gate --stage DISCOVER`.
3. Plan the questions: `aig profile plan` groups every open question by the person who should know (AI Governance, the business owner, engineering or data science, security, the vendor contact) and drafts one message per person. Nothing is sent by the draft; `--send` sends.
4. Ask AI Governance first, in the conversation, in one batched message, only their questions from the plan. Then send the rest without waiting (sending questions is do-and-tell, operating rule 13): `aig profile plan --send` writes one message per person into that person's folder (reply date five working days out; change it with `--due` or `aig request set-due`). Tell AI Governance where the messages are and who was asked what. The vendor contact is whoever AI Governance names; ask who it is.
5. Record each answer as it comes in, with its source: `aig profile set --field <id> --value "..." --source "<who or which document>"`. Several at once: `--file answers.json` with `{"field": "answer", "_source": "..."}`. File the reply with `aig request answer`. If a reply leaves some questions unanswered, use `--remaining` as before (operating rule 16).
6. The tool does the housekeeping and tells you: answers raise the factors (generative, personal data, decisions about people, and so on) but never lower them; lowering is reported and left to AI Governance as a concern. When all four screening questions are answered and no screening exists, the screening runs by itself. An answer on EU risk class of "prohibited-concern" creates a proposal for AI Governance; "not sure" creates a concern. Questions about EU duties are questions for counsel (operating rule 14): record what the company says, never what the law requires.
7. Unknown answers. Some questions will not have answers. That is allowed, but only after a named person was asked: `aig profile unknown --field <ids> --asked <person> --reason "<what they said, at least five words>"`. A question that does not apply to this system is `aig profile na --field <ids> --reason "..."`; it does not count against the limit if it is conditional, but a question that applies to every system and is skipped does.
   The limit: no more than 20 percent of the required questions may be unknown or skipped (a workspace setting). Over the limit, Discover cannot close. Say it plainly to the business: guessing wastes everyone's time, and every "not known" makes the related risks start one step worse in the assessment and creates a concern that Assess must deal with. The best way to improve the score is to ask a better person or to find the document. If an answer arrives later, record it with `aig profile set`: the concern closes by itself.
8. Watch for three things while discovering and raise them at once, in plain words, to AI Governance: (a) ongoing harm to people (for example a screening tool wrongly rejecting a group): do not fix it yourself; send the owner a request to pause or limit it, record a containment with an end date and the request it belongs to (`aig containment add --request REQ-xxx ...`), and route the lasting rule to GOVERN. Word the containment as the measure ("Switch off automatic rejection ...") and say in chat that it was decided and asked of the owner, never that it is switched off: it is a fact only when the owner says so, and then you record it with `aig containment confirm --id CT-xxx --source "<who said it>"`; (b) a possible legal hard stop (a use that may be prohibited, or a regulated use that may be missing a step, such as a bias audit or a notice to the people affected). Describe the facts and ask counsel; never state in your own voice what the law requires (operating rule 14). Place the block yourself, in the same turn in which the facts show it, with `aig stage block --reason "..."` (Discover and Assess can go on; Assess cannot close and the stages after it cannot start until AI Governance lifts the block), and tell AI Governance you did and what would lift it. The facts that trigger it: the system decides or shapes decisions about people, or is used in a regulated field; people live where specific rules may apply; and the record shows no evidence of the step (no bias audit, no notice to the people affected, no impact assessment). Do not leave it as a concern only and do not wait to be asked. The Discover gate enforces this: it refuses to close while such a system has neither a block nor a recorded reason (`aig stage block-review --reason "<at least ten words: what is known and who has been asked>"`), and the reason is shown to AI Governance on the waiting page. If you judge that no block is needed, say so and why in one line of your report. Never lift a block. When AI Governance names what would lift it, send the requests for that evidence at once (to the vendor contact, the owner, counsel) and tell them who was asked: sending requests is do-and-tell (operating rule 13), not something to ask permission for; (c) governance gaps (no inventory entry, no acceptable-use rule, no named owner, vendor contract without audit or change-notice terms): the profile's section K collects them, and GOVERN picks them up.
9. Check first: `aig project show` says whether the lane is already decided. If it is, do not ask again. Otherwise Genesis prepares the lane decision package. Never assume AI Governance knows what a lane is. The screening output prints the three lane options in plain words (`aig project lane-options` shows them again). Show them as they are, say which one you recommend and why, then ask which they choose. AI Governance decides: `aig project lane-set --lane light|standard|full --by <id> --statement "<their words>"`. The light lane is refused if any factor rules it out; if they ask for it anyway, explain the refusal in the same words and offer the nearest allowed lane. Moving a lane down needs a reason in their own words. Choosing the lane changes how many questions apply, so look at the plan again afterwards.
10. AI Governance starts the stage: `aig stage start --stage DISCOVER --by <id> --statement "<their words>"`.
11. Run `aig stage gate --stage DISCOVER`. If it lists anything, say so plainly: never report "nothing is waiting" while the gate has open items or questions are unanswered. Before you recommend closing, make sure every question is answered or recorded as not known after asking, every follow-up is sent (operating rule 16), and the unknown rate is under the limit. When the gate passes, `aig stage close --stage DISCOVER`. AI Governance may close over open items only through an informed override (operating rule 12): first list exactly what stays open and why it matters, recommend against it when it affects people or the law, and ask for their confirming reason in their own words: say what the reply must cover, and never offer a sample sentence or draft it for them. Never close on a first casual instruction such as "it is only a test".
12. Show AI Governance the regulatory view when they ask what the product covers: `aig reg project` (ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF for this system) and `aig reg coverage`. It is an indicative map, not legal advice and not a compliance statement.

## Light lane (one pass)

For the lowest tier with no ruling-out factor. Starting DISCOVER starts the combined pass: answer the core questions, then in the same sitting `aig risk init`, `aig risk set --all-defaults --reason "..."` (or rate quickly), `aig control draft` (the core-minimum set), one batch approval, assign owners, then close DISCOVER (the gate checks all three at once). Annual check plus triggers: a new factor raises the lane automatically.

## Done when

The gate passes. Nothing here involves control IDs for the owner; Atlas speaks in plain words.
